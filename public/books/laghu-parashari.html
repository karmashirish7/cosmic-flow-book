Introduction (Bhūmikā)What this book is

Sage Parashara's great astrology text, the Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra, is very long. A later scholar pulled out its most useful rules for predicting when things happen and wrote them in about 42 short verses. He called it Uḍudāya Pradīpa, "the lamp for star-based periods". People now call it Laghu Parashari, "the short Parashari".

Most astrology books judge a planet only by its natural nature: Jupiter good, Saturn bad, and so on. Parashara's key idea is that a planet also has a temporary nature, which comes from the houses it rules in a particular chart. Saturn can be the best planet in one chart and Jupiter the worst in another. Laghu Parashari teaches how to work this out and then how to read it through the planetary periods (dashas).

Pt. Sitaram Jha wrote this commentary because earlier commentaries contained errors. He gives the reason behind each rule and works examples. The edition also contains a second, shorter text, the Madhya Parashari ("the middle Parashari"), which adds yogas for kingship, wealth and poverty, and a list of good and bad planets for each rising sign.

How to read this guide

Verse numbers appear like LP 1.6 (Laghu Parashari, chapter 1, verse 6) or MP 2.10 (Madhya Parashari). The book numbers Laghu Parashari verses straight through chapter 1 (4 to 13), then restarts at 1 in each later chapter, and this guide follows that. Gold boxes hold the original Sanskrit śloka, transcribed from the scan, with the plain-English version after it. Grey boxes state a rule. Green boxes are examples. Blue boxes are notes: the commentator's reasoning, cautions, or my explanations. Where an example is mine and not the book's, it says so.

Commentator's story · why Sun and Moon rule one sign each

The Sun and Moon are the king and queen. The Sun took the six signs from Leo onward; the Moon took the six from Cancer backward. Each kept one sign for itself and gave one sign from each half to every other planet, in order of rank: the prince Mercury (Virgo and Gemini), the minister Venus (Libra and Taurus), the general Mars (Scorpio and Aries), the minister Jupiter (Sagittarius and Pisces), and last the servant Saturn (Capricorn and Aquarius). That is why every other planet owns two signs. It is a memory aid, and a good one.

Part I · Laghu ParashariUḍudāya Pradīpa, with the commentary of Pt. Sitaram Jha

Opening verses 1–3The dasha clock (Vimshottari)

After the usual prayers (to Sarasvati), the author says what the book is for: predicting results through nakshatra dashas. He names one system only.

Śloka 1 · prayer to Sarasvati

सिद्धान्तमौपनिषदं शुद्धान्तं परमेष्ठिनः ।
शोणाधरं महः किञ्चिद्वीणाधरमुपास्महे ॥१॥

We worship a certain radiance with red lips who holds the vina, the truth of the Upanishads, living in Brahma's inner palace. That is, Goddess Sarasvati.

Śloka 2 · purpose

वयं पाराशरीं होरामनुसृत्य यथामति ।
उडुदायप्रदीपाख्यं कुर्मो दैवविदां मुदे ॥२॥

Following Parashara's Hora as well as we understand it, we write this book, called "Uḍudāya Pradīpa", for the joy of astrologers.

Śloka 3 · which dasha

फलानि नक्षत्रदशाप्रकारेण विवृण्महे ।
दशा विंशोत्तरी चात्र ग्राह्या नाष्टोत्तरी मता ॥३॥

We explain results by the method of nakshatra dashas. Here the Vimshottari dasha is to be used, not the Ashtottari.

Rule LP v.3

Use the Vimshottari (120-year) dasha. Do not use the Ashtottari (108-year) dasha for these rules.

Which period you are born in

The Moon's nakshatra at birth decides the first dasha. Starting from Krittika and going round three times, the nine planets rule the 27 nakshatras in a fixed order. Each planet's period has a fixed length, and the nine add up to 120 years.

How much of the first dasha is left at birth

The book uses two old time measures: bhayāta, how much of the birth nakshatra had already passed, and bhabhoga, the full length of that nakshatra. Both are in ghatis and palas (1 ghati = 24 minutes, 1 pala = 24 seconds).

Formula

Part already used = dasha years × bhayāta ÷ bhabhoga.
Balance at birth = dasha years − part used.

In modern terms: the fraction of the nakshatra the Moon has already crossed is the fraction of the dasha already used up.

The book's example

A person is born in Shaka 1848 (1927 CE), in Magha month, bright-half Ekadashi, on a Saturday. The Moon is in Mrigashira, so the first dasha is Mars (7 years).

  • Bhayāta = 58 ghatis 15 palas = 58.25 ghatis
  • Bhabhoga = 59 ghatis 30 palas = 59.5 ghatis
  • Used = 7 × 58.25 ÷ 59.5 = 6.853 years = 6 years, 10 months, 7 days
  • Balance = 7 − 6.853 = about 1 month 22 days of Mars dasha left

So this person runs Mars for under two months, then Rahu for 18 years, then Jupiter for 16, and so on in the fixed order.

Sub-periods (antardasha) the easy way

Every major period is split into nine sub-periods, starting with the major lord itself and then following the same order. Each sub-period is the same share of the major period that its lord has of 120 years.

Formulas

Antardasha (months) = major years × minor years ÷ 10.

The book's shortcut: major years × 3 gives a "key" in days. Multiply the key by each minor lord's years to get that sub-period in days. Divide by 30 for months.

Pratyantar (days) = major years × minor years × sub-minor years ÷ 40.

Example

Sun–Sun: key = 6 × 3 = 18 days. 18 × 6 = 108 days = 3 months 18 days.
Sun–Moon: 6 × 10 ÷ 10 = 6 months.
Venus–Venus: 20 × 20 ÷ 10 = 40 months = 3 years 4 months.
Sun–Sun–Sun pratyantar: 6 × 6 × 6 ÷ 40 = 5.4 days = 5 days 24 ghatis. This matches the book's table.

Note

The book's 20 pages of tables are just these formulas worked out for every combination. The book uses a 360-day year (12 months of 30 days). Modern software usually uses a 365.25-day year, so dates can differ by a few days per decade. The calculator below uses 365.25 days.

Chapter 1 · Saṃjñā Adhyāya, verses 4–13Which planets are good or bad for your chart

This is the heart of the book. The author assumes you already know the basics (houses, signs, exaltation) from other texts. Here he gives Parashara's special rules for deciding whether a planet will give good or bad results in a particular chart.

Śloka 4

बुधैर्भावादयः सर्वे ज्ञेयाः सामान्यशास्त्रतः ।
एतच्छास्त्रानुसारेण संज्ञां ब्रूमो विशेषतः ॥४॥

The learned should know houses and all such basics from the general texts. We state only the special terms according to this teaching.

Why the houses are in this order

The commentator explains the twelve houses as a chain of human needs, each one leading to the next:

  1. Body (tanu): the sign rising at birth is the body itself.
  2. Wealth (dhana): once you have a body you need food and money to keep it.
  3. Effort, siblings (sahaja): earning money needs effort, and brothers help.
  4. Home, mother, comfort (sukha): effort brings a home and family happiness.
  5. Children, learning (suta, vidyā): next comes the wish for children and knowledge.
  6. Disease, enemies (ripu): a healthy body is needed to marry; disease is the inner enemy.
  7. Spouse (jāyā): free of disease, one marries.
  8. Death, lifespan (mṛtyu, āyu): having a family, one wants to live long.
  9. Dharma, fortune (dharma, bhāgya): righteous living extends life.
  10. Work, status (karma, rājya): dharma needs action and the support of a ruler or father.
  11. Gains (āya): action needs income.
  12. Expenses, loss (vyaya): income must be spent.

Aspects (who "sees" whom) LP 1.5

Śloka 5

पश्यन्ति सप्तमं सर्वे शनिजीवकुजाः पुनः ।
विशेषतश्च त्रिदशत्रिकोणचतुरष्टमान् ॥५॥

All planets see the 7th. Saturn, Jupiter and Mars also see, in order, the 3rd and 10th, the trines (5th and 9th), and the 4th and 8th.

Rule

Every planet fully aspects the 7th house from itself. In addition, Saturn fully aspects the 3rd and 10th, Jupiter the 5th and 9th, and Mars the 4th and 8th.

Note

Other texts give every planet partial aspects (¼, ½, ¾) on these houses. Laghu Parashari counts only full aspects. The commentator's reasons: Saturn is the servant, and servants look after effort (3rd) and the master's work (10th). Jupiter is the teacher, so he watches learning (5th) and dharma (9th). Mars is the guard, so he protects home (4th) and life (8th). All planets watch the 7th because protecting the spouse protects dharma and family.

Example (mine)

Saturn in the 1st house aspects the 3rd, 7th and 10th houses. Jupiter in the 4th aspects the 8th, 10th and 12th. Mars in the 6th aspects the 9th, 12th and 1st.

Natural good and bad LP 1.5

Sun, Saturn and Mars are natural malefics. Jupiter and Venus are natural benefics. Moon and Mercury are neutral. Rahu and Ketu give results by association. When a planet is good both naturally and by house-rulership, it is very good; bad both ways, very bad; good one way and bad the other, it evens out.

Rule 1 · Trine lords are good, 3-6-11 lords are bad LP 1.6

Śloka 6

सर्वे त्रिकोणनेतारो ग्रहाः शुभफलप्रदाः ।
पतयस्त्रिषडायानां यदि पापफलप्रदाः ॥६॥

All planets that lead the trines give good results. If they are lords of the 3rd, 6th and 11th, they give bad results.

Rule

Any planet ruling a trikona (1st, 5th, 9th) gives good results. Any planet ruling the 3rd, 6th or 11th gives bad results. Strength rises in order: 1st lord < 5th lord < 9th lord, and 3rd lord < 6th lord < 11th lord.

Commentator's reasoning

Learning (5th) and dharma (9th) make even a cruel person gentle, so even Saturn or Mars becomes helpful when it rules them. The lagna (body) counts as a trine too, because whoever controls his body is called good. On the other side, constant income (11th), great force (3rd) or enemies (6th) make even a gentle person harsh. So even Jupiter turns harmful by ruling these.

Example (mine)

Taurus lagna: Saturn rules the 9th and 10th, so Saturn becomes very good even though it is a natural malefic.
Libra lagna: Jupiter rules the 3rd and 6th, so Jupiter gives bad results despite being the greatest natural benefic.

Rule 2 · Kendra lords lose their natural nature LP 1.7

Śloka 7

न दिशन्ति शुभं नृणां सौम्याः केन्द्राधिपा यदि ।
क्रूराश्चेदशुभं ह्येते प्रबलाश्चोत्तरोत्तरम् ॥७॥

If benefics rule kendras, they do not give good results to people. If cruel planets rule them, they do not give bad results. Each later kendra is stronger than the one before.

Rule

When a natural benefic (Jupiter, Venus, full Moon, Mercury) rules a kendra (4th, 7th, 10th), it stops giving good results. When a natural malefic (Sun, Saturn, Mars, waning Moon, afflicted Mercury) rules a kendra, it stops giving bad results. This effect grows: 4th lord < 7th lord < 10th lord.

Note · "kendradhipati dosha"

The lagna is left out here, since it was already counted as a trine. The commentator's reason: someone who always has comfort (4th), a beautiful spouse (7th) or power (10th) gets absorbed in it and forgets his own nature. So kendra lords become neutral. This "fault of ruling a kendra" is the famous kendradhipati dosha.

Example (mine)

Gemini lagna: Jupiter rules the 7th and 10th, both kendras. Jupiter's natural goodness is cancelled, and it is also a maraka (7th lord). For Gemini, the book lists Jupiter as a malefic.

Rule 3 · 2nd and 12th lords depend on company LP 1.8

Śloka 8

लग्नाद्व्ययद्वितीयेशौ परेषां साहचर्यतः ।
स्थानान्तरानुगुण्येन भवतः फलदायकौ ॥८॥

The lords of the 12th and 2nd from the lagna give results according to their company with others, and according to their other house.

Rule

The lords of the 2nd and 12th have no fixed nature. They give results according to (a) the planets they sit with, and (b) the other house they rule. If they sit with no planet and rule no other house, they give their own natural result.

Commentator's reasoning

A man's money (2nd) goes the way of his companions: thieves waste it, good friends protect it. Spending (12th) is the same. So these lords follow their company. The commentator says "company" means sitting together, and "other house" means the second sign the planet owns. He rejects longer lists of relationships that other commentators add.

Rule 4 · The 8th lord is bad LP 1.9

Śloka 9

भाग्यव्ययाधिपत्येन रन्ध्रेशो न शुभप्रदः ।
स एव शुभसन्धाता लग्नाधीशोऽपि चेत्स्वयम् ॥९॥

Because it rules the "loss of fortune" (12th from the 9th), the 8th lord does not give good. The same planet brings good if it is itself also the lagna lord.

Rule

The 8th lord is not good, because the 8th is the 12th from the 9th: it "spends" your fortune. But if the 8th lord also rules the lagna, it becomes helpful.

Why the 8th is singled out

Every house is the 12th (the loss) of the house after it. So why is only the 8th lord called bad? The commentator's answer: loss of fortune (9th) hurts more than any other loss. He also walks through each house as "the spender of the next house": the 2nd lord as spender of the 3rd (strength and life), the 7th lord as spender of the 8th (life), and so on. This explains why the 2nd and 7th become killers.

Example (mine)

Aries lagna: Mars rules both the 1st and 8th. Because it also rules the lagna, Mars is not harmful here.
Gemini lagna: Saturn rules the 8th and 9th. The 9th (a trine) saves it; it gives mixed-to-good results.

How strong each fault is LP 1.10–11

Ślokas 10–11

केन्द्राधिपत्यदोषस्तु बलवान् गुरुशुक्रयोः ।
मारकत्वेऽपि च तयोर्मारकस्थानसंस्थितिः ॥१०॥

बुधस्तदनु चन्द्रोऽपि भवेत्तदनु तद्विधः ।
न रन्ध्रेशत्वदोषस्तु सूर्याचन्द्रमसोर्भवेत् ॥११॥

The fault of kendra rulership is strong for Jupiter and Venus. So is their maraka power, and their placement in a maraka house. Mercury is next, and the Moon after him. The 8th-lord fault does not apply to the Sun and Moon.

  • The kendra-lord fault is strongest for Jupiter and Venus, weaker for Mercury, and weakest for the Moon.
  • Jupiter and Venus are also the most dangerous marakas when they rule a kendra and sit in a maraka house (2nd or 7th).
  • The Sun and Moon do not suffer the 8th-lord fault. (They own only one sign, so in Leo or Cancer as the 8th they rule nothing else to spoil.)
Note

The commentator's analogy: a stain shows most on the cleanest cloth. Jupiter and Venus are the purest benefics, so the kendra fault hits them hardest.

Mars as 10th lord LP 1.12

Śloka 12

कुजस्य कर्मनेतृत्वप्रयुक्ता शुभकारिता ।
त्रिकोणस्यापि नेतृत्वे न कर्मेशत्वमात्रतः ॥१२॥

The good-giving that comes to Mars from ruling the 10th happens when it also rules a trine, not from 10th-lordship alone.

Rule

Mars ruling the 10th alone does not make it good. Ruling a kendra only removes its harm. Mars becomes actively good when it rules a trine too.

Example (mine)

Cancer lagna: Mars rules the 5th and 10th, a trine and a kendra. It becomes the yogakaraka.
Leo lagna: Mars rules the 4th and 9th, again kendra plus trine, so it is very good.
Aquarius lagna: Mars rules the 3rd and 10th. The kendra cancels some harm, but the 3rd keeps it bad. The book lists it as a malefic.

Rahu and Ketu LP 1.13

Śloka 13

यद्यद्भावगतौ वाऽपि यद्यद्भावेशसंयुतौ ।
तत्तत्फलानि प्रबलौ प्रदिशेतां तमोग्रहौ ॥१३॥

Whatever house they occupy, and whatever house lord they join, the two powerful shadow planets give those results.

Rule

Rahu and Ketu own no signs. They give results according to the house they occupy and the planet they sit with.

Note

They are points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's path, not physical bodies. So, the commentator says, they reflect whatever they are attached to.

Chapter 2 · Yoga Adhyāya, verses 1–9How yogas form

Chapter 1 sorted the planets into good and bad. Chapter 2 asks: what happens when the lord of a kendra and the lord of a trine come together? The answer is raja yoga.

The basic yoga LP 2.1–2

Ślokas 1–2

केन्द्रत्रिकोणपतयः सम्बन्धेन परस्परम् ।
इतरैरप्रसक्ताश्चेद्विशेषफलदायकाः ॥१॥

केन्द्रत्रिकोणनेतारौ दोषयुक्तावपि स्वयम् ।
सम्बन्धमात्राद्बलिनौ भवेतां योगकारकौ ॥२॥

Lords of kendras and trines, connected with each other and not tied to others, give special results. Even if they are themselves faulty, the mere connection makes them strong yogakarakas.

Rule

When a kendra lord and a trine lord are connected, they give special good results. This works best when neither is also connected to some other (bad) house lord. It works even if one of them is weak by being debilitated, combust or in an enemy sign.

Commentator's reasoning

A kendra lord has "forgotten its nature" (chapter 1). So it takes on the nature of whoever it is linked to. Linked to a trine lord, it becomes very good. Linked to a 3rd or 6th lord, it gives only ordinary results. And "a learned enemy is better than a foolish friend": once two planets are connected, each hides the other's faults.

The three kinds of connection LP 2.3

Śloka 3

निवसेतां व्यत्ययेन तावुभौ धर्मकर्मणोः ।
एकत्रान्यतरो वाऽपि वसेच्चेद्योगकारकौ ॥३॥

If the two live in each other's places (trine and kendra, "dharma and karma"), or together in one place, or one of them lives in the other's place, they become yogakarakas.

Rule · ranked strongest first
  1. Exchange: the kendra lord sits in the trine and the trine lord sits in the kendra (they swap places).
  2. Conjunction: both sit together in the same kendra or trine.
  3. One-sided: the kendra lord sits in a trine, or the trine lord sits in a kendra.
Note

Other writers list up to six kinds of connection (mutual aspect, sitting in each other's sign, and so on). Following the verse, the commentator keeps only these three placement-based ones. Many modern astrologers also count mutual aspect; just know the text itself does not mention it.

Which pairs are strongest LP 2.4

Śloka 4

त्रिकोणाधिपयोर्मध्ये सम्बन्धो येन केनचित् ।
केन्द्रनाथस्य बलिनो भवेद्यदि सुयोगकृत् ॥४॥

If the strong kendra lord (the 10th lord) is connected in any way with either of the trine lords, it makes an excellent yoga.

The 10th lord is the strongest kendra lord. A link between the 10th lord and either the 5th or 9th lord is the best yoga. Weaker pairs follow in order: 7th or 4th lord with the 9th, 5th or 1st lord.

When the yoga shows up LP 2.5

Śloka 5

दशास्वपि भवेद्योगः प्रायशो योगकारिणोः ।
दशाद्वयीमध्यगतस्तदयुक्शुभकारिणाम् ॥५॥

The yoga mostly happens within the dashas of the two yogakarakas (one's dasha, the other's sub-period). It also happens in the dashas of unconnected benefics, when the two yogakarakas run as sub-periods inside them.

Rule

A yoga gives results mainly when the dasha of one yoga planet runs with the antardasha of the other. A natural benefic that is not connected to the yoga planets can still deliver the yoga in its own dasha, during the yoga planets' sub-periods.

Note

The commentator's image: good people help others' work without wanting anything; bad people get in the way even when they gain nothing. So unconnected benefics help a yoga along, and unconnected malefics block it.

Malefics linked to yogakarakas LP 2.6

Śloka 6

योगकारकसम्बन्धात्पापिनोऽपि ग्रहाः स्वतः ।
तत्तद्भुक्त्यनुसारेण दिशेयुर्योगजं फलम् ॥६॥

A malefic connected to a yogakaraka will also give yoga results in its own dasha, during the yogakaraka's sub-periods. Even bad people do good work when they are tied to a good person.

One planet ruling both LP 2.7

Śloka 7

केन्द्रत्रिकोणाधिपयोरैक्ये चेद्योगकारिता ।
अन्यत्रिकोणपतिना सम्बन्धो यदि किं परम् ॥७॥

Rule

A single planet that rules both a kendra and a trine is a yogakaraka by itself. If it is also connected to another trine lord, nothing is better.

Example (mine)

Natural yogakarakas by lagna: Taurus and Libra have Saturn. Cancer and Leo have Mars. Capricorn and Aquarius have Venus. The other six lagnas have no single planet ruling both kinds of house.

Rahu and Ketu as yogakarakas LP 2.8

Śloka 8

यदि केन्द्रे त्रिकोणे वा निवसेतां तमोग्रहौ ।
नाथेनान्यतरेणापि सम्बन्धाद्योगकारकौ ॥८॥

If Rahu or Ketu sits in a kendra and is connected to a trine lord, or sits in a trine and is connected to a kendra lord, it becomes a yogakaraka. A blind man walks the road his guide shows him; Rahu and Ketu follow whoever they are tied to.

When the yoga breaks LP 2.9

Śloka 9

धर्मकर्माधिनेतारौ रन्ध्रलाभाधिपौ यदि ।
तयोः सम्बन्धमात्रेण न योगं लभते नरः ॥९॥

Rule

If the 9th lord also rules the 8th, or the 10th lord also rules the 11th, a simple connection between them does not give yoga.

Note

The "fault" that LP 2.2 says can be ignored means weakness (debilitation, enemy sign). Ruling a bad house is a different fault, and it does break the yoga. The commentator points out that the man who knows dharma but also destroys dharma, or who does his duty but also wastes his gains, cannot bring growth.

Example (mine)

Gemini lagna: Saturn rules the 8th and 9th; Jupiter rules the 7th and 10th. A Saturn–Jupiter link here is not a dependable raja yoga. The Madhya Parashari says the same thing (see MP 8).
Pisces lagna: Jupiter rules the 1st and 10th, Mars the 2nd and 9th. A Jupiter–Mars link is a strong yoga, and the book names both as rajayoga planets for Pisces.

Chapter 3 · Āyurvicāra Adhyāya, verses 1–6Longevity and the "killer" planets

Read this first

This chapter talks about death directly. In practice, maraka periods are read today as periods of serious health risk, crisis or major endings, not certain death. Lifespan is also estimated by other methods first. Never use these rules to frighten anyone.

Houses of life and houses of death LP 3.1

Śloka 1½

अष्टमं ह्यायुषः स्थानमष्टमादष्टमं च यत् ।
तयोरपि व्ययस्थानं मारकस्थानमुच्यते ॥१॥

तत्राप्याद्यव्ययस्थानाद्द्वितीयं बलवत्तरम् ।

The 8th is the house of life, and so is the 8th from the 8th (the 3rd). The 12th from each of them is called a maraka house. Of these, the 2nd is stronger than the first loss-house (the 7th).

Rule

The 8th house is the house of life. The 3rd (8th from the 8th) is also a house of life. The 12th house from each of these, the 7th and the 2nd, are the maraka houses. Of the two, the 2nd is stronger.

Commentator's reasoning

Without courage and strength (3rd), life is like death, so the 3rd is a house of life. The houses that "spend" life (12th from them) become the killers. Losing strength (2nd = 12th from 3rd) hurts more than losing the life-span house alone, so the 2nd is stronger.

Who brings death and when LP 3.2–3

Ślokas 2–3

तदीशितुस्तत्र गताः पापिनस्तेन संयुताः ॥२॥

तेषां दशाविपाकेषु सम्भवे निधनं नृणाम् ।
तेषामसम्भवे साक्षाद्व्ययाधीशदशास्वपि ॥३॥

The lords of these houses, malefics placed in them, and malefics joined with those lords: in their dashas and sub-periods, when the time is ripe, people die. If none of these applies, it can happen in the dashas of the 12th lord.

Rule · marakas, strongest first
  1. The 2nd lord
  2. The 7th lord
  3. Malefics sitting in the 2nd
  4. Malefics sitting with the 2nd lord
  5. Malefics sitting in the 7th
  6. Malefics sitting with the 7th lord

Death can come in the dasha of one maraka with the antardasha of another, but only when the lifespan already worked out by other methods is complete. If none of these applies, look at the dasha of the 12th lord with a maraka's sub-period.

Note

The commentator adds: the Nakshatra-based lifespan in Kali Yuga is a guide, not a fixed date. Death can come a little before or after, when a strong maraka period arrives.

Backup marakas LP 3.4–5

Ślokas 4–5

अलाभे पुनरेतेषां सम्बन्धेन व्ययेशितुः ।
क्वचिच्छुभानां च दशास्वष्टमेशदशासु च ॥४॥

केवलानां च पापानां दशासु निधनं क्वचित् ।
कल्पनीयं बुधैर्नृणां मारकाणामदर्शने ॥५॥

If no maraka period falls at the right time, death may come in the period of (a) benefics connected to the 12th lord, (b) the 8th lord, or (c) plain malefics with no maraka connection. The astrologer has to judge.

Saturn overrides all LP 3.6

Śloka 6

मारकैः सह सम्बन्धान्निहन्ता पापकृच्छनिः ।
अतिक्रम्येतरान् सर्वान् भवत्येव न संशयः ॥६॥

Rule

If Saturn is a functional malefic (by ruling a bad house) and is connected to the marakas, Saturn becomes the main killer, ahead of all the others.

Example (mine)

Aries lagna: Venus rules both the 2nd (Taurus) and 7th (Libra), so Venus is the prime maraka. The Madhya Parashari confirms Venus as the "direct killer" for Aries. Saturn rules the 10th and 11th, so it is a functional malefic for Aries; if Saturn is linked to Venus, Saturn's period would be the most dangerous.

Chapter 4 · Daśāphala Adhyāya, verses 1–8How a dasha actually gives its results

Planets act through their friends LP 4.1–2

Ślokas 1–2

न दिशेयुर्ग्रहाः सर्वे स्वदशासु स्वभुक्तिषु ।
शुभाशुभफलं नृणामात्मभावानुरूपतः ॥१॥

आत्मसम्बन्धिनो ये च ये वा निजसधर्मिणः ।
तेषामन्तर्दशास्वेव दिशन्ति स्वदशाफलम् ॥२॥

Rule

A planet does not give its full result in its own sub-period inside its own dasha. It gives its full result in the sub-periods of planets that are (a) connected to it, or (b) of the same nature (both good or both bad, by rulership).

Commentator's image

When relatives or like-minded friends visit, people bring out their best things and show them off. When the guests leave, life goes back to normal. A planet behaves the same way when "its people" arrive as sub-period lords.

Sub-periods of unrelated planets LP 4.3

Śloka 3

इतरेषां दशानाथविरुद्धफलदायिनाम् ।
तत्तत्फलानुगुण्येन फलान्यूह्यानि सूरिभिः ॥३॥

For sub-period lords that are neither connected nor alike, blend the nature of the dasha lord and the sub-period lord. The commentator gives six types, from best to worst: connected and alike, connected and neutral, connected and opposite, unconnected and alike, unconnected and opposite, unconnected and neutral.

Kendra and trine lords together LP 4.4

Śloka 4

स्वदशायां त्रिकोणेशभुक्तौ केन्द्रपतिः शुभम् ।
दिशेत्सोऽपि तथा नो चेदसम्बन्धेन पापकृत् ॥४॥

Rule

A kendra lord gives good results in its own dasha during the sub-period of a connected trine lord. The trine lord does the same in its dasha during the connected kendra lord's sub-period. If they are not connected, both give bad results in each other's sub-periods.

How raja yoga unfolds over time LP 4.5–6

Ślokas 5–6

आरम्भो राजयोगस्य भवेन्मारकभुक्तिषु ।
प्रथयन्ति तमारभ्य क्रमशः पापभुक्तयः ॥५॥

तत्सम्बन्धिशुभानां तु तथा पुनरसंयुजाम् ।
शुभानां तु समत्वेन संयोगो योगकारिणाम् ॥६॥

Rule

In a yogakaraka's dasha, the raja yoga begins in the sub-period of a connected maraka, and then grows step by step through the sub-periods of malefics that follow. In the sub-periods of benefics (connected or not), the yoga arrives all at once, the way the yogakaraka itself gives it.

Other cases LP 4.7–8

Ślokas 7–8

शुभस्यास्य प्रसक्तस्य दशायां योगकारकाः ।
स्वभुक्तिषु प्रयच्छन्ति कुत्रचिद्योगजं फलम् ॥७॥

तमोग्रहौ शुभारूढावसम्बन्धेन केनचित् ।
अन्तर्दशानुसारेण भवेतां योगकारकौ ॥८॥

  • In the dasha of a benefic connected to a yogakaraka, the yogakaraka sometimes gives yoga in its own sub-period.
  • Rahu or Ketu in a trine, even with no connection, gives yoga in its sub-period within the dasha of a yogakaraka.

When a maraka strikes LP 5.3

Śloka 3

सत्यपि स्वेन सम्बन्धे न हन्ति शुभभुक्तिषु ।
हन्ति सत्यप्यसम्बन्धे मारकः पापभुक्तिषु ॥३॥

Rule

A maraka does not kill in its own dasha during a benefic's sub-period, even if they are connected. It does kill during a malefic's sub-period, even if they are not connected.

Saturn and Venus swap results LP 5.4

Śloka 4

परस्परदशायां स्वभुक्तौ सूर्यजभार्गवौ ।
व्यत्ययेन विशेषेण प्रदिशेतां शुभाशुभम् ॥४॥

In Venus dasha, Saturn's sub-period gives Venus's results. In Saturn dasha, Venus's sub-period gives Saturn's results. This is especially strong when both are powerful.

Example (mine)

Taurus lagna: Saturn is the yogakaraka, Venus is the lagna lord but also the 6th lord. In Venus dasha, Saturn's sub-period can bring the problems of Venus's 6th-house side (debt, disease, disputes), even though Saturn is the best planet for the chart. In Saturn dasha, Venus's sub-period can bring Saturn's yoga results.

Two raja yogas with the lagna lord LP 5.5–6

Ślokas 5–6

कर्मलग्नाधिनेतारावन्योन्याश्रयसंस्थितौ ।
राजयोगाविति प्रोक्तं विख्यातो विजयी भवेत् ॥५॥

राजयोगाविति प्रोक्तं विख्यातो विजयी भवेत् ॥६॥

Rule

(a) Lagna lord and 10th lord in each other's houses, or together in the 1st or 10th: raja yoga. The person becomes famous and victorious.
(b) Lagna lord and 9th lord in each other's houses, or together in the 1st or 9th: raja yoga, with the same result.

These verses show that the lagna lord counts as both a kendra lord and a trine lord. The commentator's point: every house serves the body (lagna). A house lord cannot give its full result until it connects with the lagna lord.

The yoga that works. Venus, the 5th lord (a trine), is exalted in the 10th (a kendra). It sits with Mercury, who rules the 1st and 4th (a kendra). This is a trine lord and a kendra lord together in a kendra, the second kind of link in LP 2.3. Mercury is debilitated, but LP 2.2 says weakness does not cancel the link. MP 5.12 and 5.23 promise success in the dasha of a lagna lord joined with the 5th lord, and of a 5th lord in the 10th. So the yoga's timing points to Venus dasha, 1895–1915.

Rule

Bad sum: add the exact longitudes of the 11th, 3rd, 8th, 6th and 12th houses. Remove whole multiples of 12 signs. The house where the result falls is harmed.

Good sum: do the same with the four kendras (1, 4, 7, 10), or with the three trines (1, 5, 9). The house where the result falls is helped, even if it is a bad house.

Among kendras, the 4th and 10th are best; among trines, the 5th and 9th.

Note

The result is written as "signs completed, degrees, minutes, seconds". So 0 signs means Aries, 3 signs means Cancer, and so on. See the worked chart above for the book's example.

Commentator's correction

In verses 15–16, another printed edition read dhana as the 2nd house and tatra as "there". This gave the self-contradicting rule that benefics in the 2nd give wealth but Mercury and Jupiter there destroy it. The commentator reads dhana as the 9th house (as MP 1.3 defines it) and decodes tvatra with the katapayadi number code as 24, which is 12 after removing 12. So: benefics in the 9th give wealth, and Jupiter-Mercury in the 12th destroys it. This makes sense, since the 12th "spends" whatever its planets signify.

Rule MP 7.6, 7.9–15

A strong karaka helps its house and its people; a weak one harms them. In a planet's dasha, gains come through the people of the planet whose divisional sign the dasha lord occupies. In the Sun's division, through rulers or the father. In the Moon's, through the mother. In Mars's, through sons or leaders. In Mercury's, through siblings or princes. In Jupiter's, through teachers or ministers. In Venus's, through women. In Saturn's, through servants.

Note

Verses 2–3 and 15 assign caste labels to planets and signs (Jupiter and Venus brahmin, Sun and Mars kshatriya, and so on). These reflect the society of the time. Today they are read as types of work and temperament: teachers and advisers, leaders and soldiers, traders, labourers.

Final rules MP 8.31–32

All planets except the Sun and Moon can kill when they are maraka lords. A maraka does not kill in its own sub-period within its own dasha. It kills in the sub-period of a malefic, never in a benefic's sub-period.

How the verdicts follow the rules (Aries)

Saturn rules the 10th and 11th: the kendra removes its harm, but the 11th keeps it bad. Mercury rules the 3rd and 6th: bad. Venus rules the 2nd and 7th, both maraka houses, so it is the prime killer. The Sun rules the 5th: good. Jupiter rules the 9th and 12th: the 9th makes it good. But a Saturn–Jupiter link does not give yoga, because Jupiter is also the 12th lord. The Moon (4th) and Mars (1st and 8th) are neutral.

My notes for studyReading notes and common pitfalls

1 · Rulership beats nature

The single most important lesson: always ask "which houses does this planet rule in this chart?" before calling it good or bad. A natural benefic can be your worst planet and a natural malefic your best.

2 · Trine wins over everything

When a planet rules two houses with opposite results, the trine side usually wins. A planet ruling a trine and the 8th (e.g. Saturn for Gemini) leans good. A planet ruling a kendra and a 3/6/11 house (e.g. Mars for Aquarius) stays bad.

3 · The dasha lord sets the stage, the sub-lord delivers

Chapters 4 and 5 boil down to this: a major period only "shows" its promise in the sub-periods of planets linked to it or of the same nature. When you time an event, look at the dasha–antardasha pair, not the dasha alone.

4 · Connection is narrow in this text

The commentator counts only exchange, conjunction and one-sided placement. Many modern Parashari astrologers also use mutual aspect. If you use it, note that you are going beyond the text.

5 · The book has small internal tensions

For Leo lagna, chapter 8 calls Mars "good" but does not call it a yogakaraka, even though by LP 2.7 Mars (4th and 9th lord) should be one. Libra lagna names Moon and Mercury (10th and 9th lords) as the raja yoga pair rather than Saturn. Treat chapter 8 as a quick list and the Laghu Parashari rules as the method underneath.

6 · Use the death and caste verses responsibly

Maraka periods signal health risk, endings or crisis. They do not fix a death date. Caste and animal lists reflect their era; translate them into modern meaning or set them aside.

Kendra lord + trine lord connected, or one planet ruling both. Best: 10th lord with 9th or 5th lord.

Exchange > conjunction > one in the other's house.

Yoga breaks

9th lord also ruling the 8th, or 10th lord also ruling the 11th.

2nd and 7th lords, planets in them, and planets with their lords. The 2nd is stronger. Saturn as malefic + maraka link overrides all.

Results come in sub-periods of connected or same-natured planets. Raja yoga starts in a maraka's sub-period and grows through malefic ones.

Kills in a malefic's sub-period, not in a benefic's, and not in its own.
